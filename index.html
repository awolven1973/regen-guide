A developing, college-level guide to Regen as a declarative, knowledge-based generative programming environment. Learn how objects encode relationships, design intent, composition, and protocols. Assumes familiarity with Common Lisp.

Editorial scope. Chapters 1–2 introduce concepts. Chapter 3 presents defobject examples checked against Adhoc source patterns but not yet executed in a live Regen image. Proposed features are identified separately.

Conceptual foundations

Declarative programming expresses required properties and relationships rather than scripting every construction step. Design intent captures the reasons and requirements behind those relationships. Regen is framed as a knowledge-based system, informed by knowledge representation, ontologies, and schemas. These concepts will receive deeper treatment as the guide develops.

Planned progression

  1. Attribute evaluation and assignment. Computed values, explicit overrides, and invalidation.
  2. Composition and dependency graphs. Separate instance hierarchy from computation dependencies.
  3. Reference systems and geometry. Coordinate systems and geometric transformations.
  4. Consistency and recovery. Existing behavior and proposed transactional mechanisms, clearly distinguished.
  5. Protocols and backends. Interfaces, conformance, and mappings to independent implementations.
  6. Design intent and declarative knowledge modeling. Requirements, rationale, knowledge-based engineering, ontologies, schemas, and explicit representation of intent as a first-class concept.

How to read

Each chapter has permanent section links. Definitions link into the terminology index, and index entries lead back to the most relevant explanatory sections. The guide is fully static, so the files can also be read offline.
